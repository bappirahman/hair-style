An induction cut is clippers with no guard, run over the whole head. It leaves a shadow rather than hair — enough to show colour and the shape of your hairline, not enough to have length in any meaningful sense. Below it there is only the razor.

The name comes from military induction, where it is issued to recruits for hygiene and uniformity. Outside that context, people choose it for two reasons: because it is genuinely the simplest way to have hair, or because it looks severe and they want that.

## Who it suits

The same rules as a buzz cut, but with no margin. Oval, square and diamond faces carry it. Strong jawlines and well-shaped skulls are effectively prerequisites, because there is nothing else in the frame.

Any unevenness in scalp tone, scarring, or a pronounced occipital bump will be fully visible. That is not a reason to avoid it — plenty of people wear it precisely because they are not hiding anything — but it is worth knowing before you sit down.

If your hair is thinning, this is one of the most flattering options available. At zero length there is no distinction between dense and sparse areas, so the thinning simply stops existing as a visual problem.

## Getting the cut right

There is little to specify beyond the neckline and the hairline. Ask for the nape to be tapered rather than blocked; at this length a hard square edge at the back looks stark.

Decide whether you want the front hairline left natural or lined up. Natural is the usual choice — a sharp line-up on an induction cut can read as very aggressive, though that is sometimes exactly the intent.

## Styling it day to day

None. What replaces it is skin care, and at this length it genuinely matters. Your scalp is exposed to sunlight it has never seen, and sunburn on a shadow-length scalp is both painful and obvious. Sunscreen in summer is the single most useful habit.

A light moisturiser keeps the tone even. Uneven, flaky scalp is the main thing that makes an induction cut look unkempt, since there is no hair to distract from it.

## Living with it

Nothing degrades, because there is no shape. Stubble becomes visible after three or four days and the cut reads as "grown out" rather than "wrong" for weeks after that.

Most people who wear it settle into a fortnightly routine with their own clippers. It is one of the few cuts here where the barber is genuinely optional, and where doing it yourself produces an essentially identical result.
