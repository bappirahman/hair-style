A temple fade is the most surgically targeted cut here. Rather than shortening a band around the head, it fades only the corners at the temples — the small triangular areas where your hairline turns from the front of your head toward the sides. Everything else keeps its length.

The point is definition without removal. Fading those two corners sharpens the frame around your face and makes the hairline read as deliberate, while the sides and back stay exactly as they were. It is the reason the cut is popular with people growing their hair out who still want a defined look.

## Who it suits

Because so little is removed, it does not fight any face shape. Round faces gain from the sharpened corners, which introduce angles where the face has few. Square faces get the corners emphasised. Heart-shaped faces do well because the fade breaks up the width of the forehead.

It is at its strongest on curly and coily hair, where the contrast between faded skin at the corner and dense texture immediately above it is unmistakable. On straight hair the effect is real but subtler, and reads mostly as a very precise line-up.

## Getting the cut right

Be clear about the scope. "Just the corners at the temples — don't take it round the sides or the back" prevents the fade creeping into a standard taper, which is the most common way this cut goes wrong.

Then decide how sharp the line-up should be. A hard, ruler-straight edge across the forehead is the classic pairing and is what most people picture. A softer, more natural edge grows out more gracefully but gives up much of the effect.

## Styling it day to day

The corners are the feature, so the top should support rather than compete. A textured, forward-styled top is the usual pairing — a French crop or a short textured fringe sits naturally above a temple fade.

Keep product light and matte. Anything glossy draws attention away from the corners and toward the top, which inverts the intended emphasis.

## Living with it

Two to three weeks. Small, sharply defined areas show regrowth quickly, and a temple fade's whole value is in its precision. When the corners soften and the line-up blurs, the effect disappears even though the rest of the hair still looks fine.

The advantage is that a touch-up is quick and cheap. Many barbers will re-line the temples between full cuts, which keeps the look sharp on a schedule that suits a longer overall haircut.
