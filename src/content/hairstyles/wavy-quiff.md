A wavy quiff is front volume achieved mostly by the hair itself. Wave provides both lift and separation naturally, so instead of building height from flat hair with powder and a round brush, you are simply directing volume that is already there.

For anyone with wavy hair who has struggled to get a quiff to hold, this is usually the answer: the problem was never hold, it was that the cut and the product were working against the wave rather than with it.

## Who it suits

Round faces get useful vertical from the front lift. Square faces get the softness of wave against a defined jaw. Heart and diamond faces both benefit from volume drawing the eye upward.

Oval faces have no constraints. Oblong faces should keep the quiff low — wave already adds some height, and a tall front on a long face is unbalancing.

Wavy hair is the requirement in the name. Loose curls work well. Thick straight hair can be styled into the same shape but it becomes a standard quiff, needing more product and heat.

## Getting the cut right

Ask for good length at the front, graduating shorter toward the crown. This is the same requirement as any quiff — the front needs to be long enough to lift and fold back.

Ask specifically for no thinning. Thinning shears in wavy hair break the wave pattern into short frizzy pieces, and the entire premise of this cut is that the wave is doing the work.

Sides can be tapered or faded. A mid fade gives a sharp contrast that suits the textured top; a taper keeps it softer.

## Styling it day to day

Sea salt spray on damp hair, then dry with the airflow pointed upward at the front section. Once dry, push the front back with a flat palm in a single motion.

Use a cream rather than a clay. Wavy hair already has structure, and heavy matte products weigh down the wave that provides the volume. A light cream adds hold without flattening.

Do not touch it repeatedly while it dries. That is what produces frizz instead of definition.

## Living with it

Five weeks comfortably. Wave disguises growth well, and the quiff shape holds as the length increases — up to a point.

The limit is when the front becomes too long and heavy to lift, at which point the wave falls forward instead of standing. That is the signal, and it comes slowly.
