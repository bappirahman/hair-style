Beach waves are medium-length hair worn loose, textured and deliberately undone — the way hair looks after a day in salt water and sun. The style is defined by an absence of styling rather than a technique, which makes it either the easiest look here or a frustrating one depending on what your hair naturally does.

The cut is soft layers and no precision anywhere. The routine is salt spray and air drying. There is genuinely not much more to it, and that is the appeal.

## Who it suits

Square faces gain from the softness. Round faces benefit from the vertical fall and the irregular outline, which breaks up circularity. Diamond faces do well with the volume distributed loosely rather than concentrated.

Oval faces have no constraints. Heart faces work with the length kept around the jaw. Oblong faces are the weakest fit — medium length with vertical fall lengthens an already long face, though waves add some useful width.

Wavy hair is the natural home. Loose curls work. Thick straight hair gets a decent version with salt spray, though it will read as textured rather than genuinely wavy. Fine hair struggles, since the whole look depends on visible body.

## Getting the cut right

Ask for soft layers and no precise lines anywhere. This is a cut where the brief is essentially "don't make it neat," and it is worth saying, because most barbers default to clean shapes.

Length matters: ten to fifteen centimetres. Below that, a wave does not have room to form a visible bend and the hair just looks unbrushed.

Do not have it thinned. As with any textured cut, thinning creates short pieces that break the pattern.

## Styling it day to day

Salt spray on soaking wet hair, generously. Scrunch upward with your hands a few times. Then leave it completely alone until it is dry.

That last part is the technique. Touching hair as it air dries disrupts whatever pattern is forming, and the result is frizz rather than waves. Spray, scrunch, walk away.

If you want more definition, plaiting the damp hair loosely and letting it dry that way produces a much stronger wave, including on straight hair.

## Living with it

Eight weeks or more between cuts. Because there is no defined shape to lose, growth simply means longer waves.

The consideration is condition. Salt spray is drying if used daily on already-dry hair, so keep conditioner in the routine and apply the spray to wet rather than dry hair. Waves in poor condition read as frizz rather than texture, and that distinction is the whole style.
