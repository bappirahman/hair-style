A crew cut is short, tapered, and just long enough at the front to do something with. That last part is what separates it from a buzz cut: the length graduates from very short at the nape to two or three centimetres at the hairline, which gives the cut a front, a shape and a direction.

It has been in continuous use since the 1930s, which tells you most of what you need to know about its reliability. It is short enough to be almost maintenance-free and long enough to be a haircut rather than a decision to remove hair.

## Who it suits

Square and oval faces are the traditional fit. The short sides frame a defined jaw well and the front length keeps the proportions balanced. Round faces do better here than in a buzz cut, because the standing front section adds useful vertical height.

Diamond faces work well — the taper narrows the sides where the cheekbones are widest. Heart and oblong faces are less natural: the exposed forehead can emphasise width up top on a heart face, and the vertical front adds length to an already long one.

Fine hair is well suited, because the short length makes density irrelevant and the front section can be lifted for the impression of volume. Thick straight hair is the classic pairing. Very curly hair tends to want either a shorter, uniform cut or a longer one with defined texture — a crew cut sits awkwardly in between.

## Getting the cut right

Two numbers define it: how short the sides go and how long the front stays. "Crew cut, number one on the sides, leave three centimetres at the front" is a complete brief.

Specify that you want the top to graduate from back to front rather than being cut level. A flat-topped crew cut is a distinct and much more military look; most people asking for a crew cut want the gradual version.

## Styling it day to day

The whole cut lives in the front section. Dry it upward and slightly back rather than letting it fall flat — a hairdryer for twenty seconds makes a bigger difference than any product will.

Then a fingertip of matte clay, worked only through the front. Applying product to the tapered sides adds nothing and shows up immediately as shine on hair that is too short to hold it.

## Living with it

Four to five weeks. The sides grow slowly in visual terms because they were tapered rather than lined, and the front simply gets a little longer, which many people prefer at weeks two and three.

The point at which it stops working is when the front is long enough to fall forward instead of standing. That is your cue, and it is usually the only cue you get.
