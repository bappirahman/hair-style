A wavy crop is a short forward-styled cut that leaves enough length for natural wave to show. It is not a different shape from a textured crop so much as a different approach — the cut is built around what the hair already does rather than around what product can make it do.

That is most of the point. People with wavy hair frequently spend years fighting it, cutting it too short to wave or drying it straight. Cut to the right length and left alone, wavy hair produces texture that no styling product replicates.

## Who it suits

Broadly. Round faces do well because wave adds vertical and irregular movement rather than a horizontal line. Oblong faces gain the width that wave provides at the sides. Square faces are softened. Heart faces get the forehead broken up.

Oval faces have no issue. Diamond faces should keep the sides shorter so the wave volume does not add width right at the cheekbones.

Wavy hair is the obvious fit. Loose curls work too. Thick straight hair can carry the shape but will need salt spray to have any texture at all — at which point it is a textured crop rather than a wavy one.

## Getting the cut right

The critical thing is length. Wavy hair needs around four centimetres minimum for a wave to form a visible bend. Cut shorter, and the hair reads as slightly untidy straight hair rather than as wavy.

Ask your barber not to thin it. Thinning shears in wavy hair produce short pieces that stick out of the wave pattern and read as frizz. If the bulk is a problem, adjusting length and shape solves it more cleanly.

Point-cutting the ends is fine and useful — it encourages the wave to separate.

## Styling it day to day

The routine is mostly about what not to do. Do not blow dry with a brush, and do not pull the hair while drying — both mechanically straighten the wave you are trying to keep.

Sea salt spray on damp hair, scrunched upward with your hands, then air dried or diffused on low. That is it. A trace of light cream afterwards if you want more definition, worked in from underneath.

## Living with it

Five to six weeks, and often longer. Wave disguises growth better than almost any other texture, because the shape was never precise and a bit more length simply means slightly bigger waves.

The point to book is when the wave starts to sit heavy rather than lift — that usually means the weight has grown past what the shape can support.
