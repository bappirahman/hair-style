Medium-length hair pushed back with your hands rather than combed. The direction is the same as a slick back; everything else is different. The finish is matte instead of glossy, the surface is separated instead of even, and the routine takes about ninety seconds instead of five minutes.

It is the version of swept-back hair most people actually want. The slick back's formality suits some occasions and some faces; this suits most days.

## Who it suits

Square and diamond faces do well, since sweeping back exposes bone structure. Round faces get useful height at the front. Oval faces have no constraints.

Oblong faces should be careful — a fully exposed forehead lengthens the face, though the softer, less severe version is more forgiving than a true slick back. Heart faces have a similar consideration with forehead width.

The hairline caution applies here too, though less acutely than with a slicked finish. A matte, separated sweep draws less attention to the hairline than a smooth glossy one.

Thick and wavy hair both work well. Straight hair is fine. Curly hair does not sweep back convincingly at this length.

## Getting the cut right

Eight to twelve centimetres on top — enough to reach the crown when pushed back, not so much that it collapses forward under its own weight.

Ask for the sides to be tapered with some weight left. Very short sides under a medium top starts becoming an undercut, which is a different and more demanding cut.

Ask for graduation from front to back so the front lifts slightly rather than lying flat. That small amount of lift is what stops the style reading as hair that has simply been pushed out of the way.

## Styling it day to day

Rough dry backward with your fingers. No brush — a brush produces the even surface of a slick back, which is not what this is.

A matte cream worked through the length gives enough hold to keep the direction without any shine. Then pull a few pieces loose at the front so the surface is broken. Ninety seconds, start to finish.

## Living with it

Six weeks. This is one of the lower-maintenance medium styles: the sides are tapered rather than faded so they grow out invisibly, and the top improves for the first few weeks after a cut.

The end point is weight. When the top becomes heavy enough to fall forward rather than staying back, it needs some length or some internal bulk removed.
