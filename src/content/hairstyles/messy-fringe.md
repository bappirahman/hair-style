A messy fringe is hair left long at the front, cut so that the ends break into separate pieces, and pushed forward without much care about where each piece lands. The whole point is that it should not look arranged.

It sits somewhere between a textured crop and curtains — longer and less structured than the first, more forward-facing than the second. It has become one of the default requests for anyone who wants their hair to look current without wanting to think about it.

## Who it suits

Almost universally. Oblong and long faces gain from the horizontal break across the forehead. Round faces do better here than with a blunt fringe, because the uneven, piecey edge adds no hard horizontal line. Square faces get softened. Heart faces get the forehead width broken up.

Oval faces have no issue at all. Diamond faces are the only mild caution, since a forward fringe can add width at the temples, but even that is marginal.

Thick and wavy hair are the strongest matches. Curly hair works well if the curls are loose. Fine hair can carry it provided the texturing is light — heavily point-cut fine hair reads as thin rather than piecey, which is the main failure mode.

## Getting the cut right

Ask for the fringe to be point-cut hard. That is the technique that produces the broken edge, and asking for it explicitly matters because a barber cutting conservatively will leave it too blunt.

Do not over-specify length. The style is meant to be imprecise, and a fringe cut to a millimetre defeats it. "Long enough to reach my eyebrows, cut so it separates" is the right level of instruction.

## Styling it day to day

Sea salt spray on damp hair, push it forward with your hands, leave it to dry. That is genuinely the routine.

If you want more definition, warm a trace of matte clay between dry fingertips and pull it through the fringe ends only — not the roots. The most common way this style goes wrong is too much product, which turns pieces into clumps and reads as unwashed rather than casual.

## Living with it

Six weeks or more. There is no precise shape to lose, so growth simply extends the fringe until it starts sitting in your eyes.

That is the only real limit, and even then many people just push it sideways and carry on. It is the most forgiving style in this catalogue by a comfortable margin.
