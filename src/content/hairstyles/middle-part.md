A middle part is what curtains grow into. The parting stays central, but the length increases until the hair falls past the cheekbones and back along the sides of the head rather than forward around the face. It stops being a fringe arrangement and becomes a hairstyle with a parting.

It is one of the defining medium-length shapes of the past few years, and it has the useful property of requiring almost nothing once you have grown into it.

## Who it suits

Round faces do very well — the vertical fall either side narrows the face substantially at this length. Square faces get softened at the jaw where the hair ends. Diamond faces benefit from coverage over the cheekbones.

Oval faces have no issue. Heart-shaped faces should be a little careful, since hair falling inward toward a narrow chin can exaggerate the taper. Oblong faces are the poorest match, as a central line adds vertical emphasis to an already long face.

Thick hair is the strongest fit: a central parting halves your density, so each side needs enough to look full. Wavy hair adds welcome movement. Fine hair can look sparse either side of the line, though a slightly off-centre parting helps considerably.

## Getting the cut right

Ask for weight to be removed underneath rather than off the ends. At this length the problem is usually bulk sitting on the shoulders of the style, and internal thinning solves it without shortening the fall.

Ask for a clean centre line to be cut in rather than just combed in — your barber can cut to the parting so the two sides are balanced. Hair cut without reference to the parting always sits slightly wrong on one side.

## Styling it day to day

Set the parting while the hair is soaking wet, before you do anything else. Then dry with your head tipped down to build root volume at the crown. A middle part that fails almost always fails at the roots, not the ends — flat crown, heavy fall.

At this length, finish with a light oil through the ends rather than a clay at the roots. The ends are the oldest hair on your head and they need condition more than the roots need hold.

## Living with it

Eight weeks between shaping cuts, and the parting itself needs no maintenance at all once it is established — hair trained to part centrally will keep doing it.

The consideration is condition rather than shape. Medium-length hair shows split ends and dryness in a way short hair never does, so the routine shifts from styling products toward conditioning ones.
