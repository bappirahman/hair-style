A man bun is long hair gathered and tied, either high at the crown or low at the nape. It is less a haircut than a way of wearing one, and it is the most practical answer to what to do with hair past the shoulder.

The high version reads as deliberate and slightly formal. The low version is quieter and works in more settings. Both need the same underlying thing: enough length in the front sections to reach the back without falling out.

## Who it suits

Pulling hair back exposes the face completely, so bone structure carries the look. Square and diamond faces do well. Oval faces are fine. Heart faces work, with the exposed forehead balanced by the volume behind.

Round faces are the weakest fit — removing all the hair from around the face leaves nothing to narrow it, and a high bun adds height that reads as a ball on top. A low bun at the nape works considerably better.

Hairlines matter here more than face shape. A bun puts your hairline centre stage and pulls tension onto it. If your temples have receded, this is one of the least forgiving ways to wear hair.

Thick, wavy and curly hair all work. Fine hair produces a small bun, which is fine but often not what people picture.

## Getting the cut right

There is very little cutting involved. Ask for the ends to be tidied and for nothing to be taken off the front — front layers are exactly what determines whether a bun holds, and a barber removing "a bit of face-framing" will cost you months.

If you want a sharper silhouette, an undercut underneath removes bulk and gives the bun a much cleaner outline. It is the most common pairing and it works well.

## Styling it day to day

Tie it damp rather than soaking. Wet hair is at its weakest and tying it under tension while saturated causes breakage.

Move the tie point around. The single most common self-inflicted problem with long hair is breakage at exactly the spot where the elastic sits every day. Alternating between a high and low bun, and using a soft fabric tie rather than a rubber band, prevents it.

A little cream through the front section keeps flyaways down without making the hair look greasy.

## Living with it

The cut needs almost nothing — a shaping trim every three months to keep the ends healthy.

What it needs instead is conditioning and sensible tying. Traction at the hairline from tight, repeated tying is a genuine risk and the reason to keep buns loose. Done sensibly, this is among the lowest-effort ways to wear long hair.
