The Caesar is a short, even cut with the top combed forward and the fringe cut straight across the forehead. It appears on Roman busts two thousand years old and has never fully left circulation, which is a reasonable argument for its durability.

Everything about it is geometric. The top is a consistent length rather than graduated, the fringe is level rather than piecey, and the sides are tapered short without a fade. It is the most architectural of the fringe cuts and the least dependent on styling.

## Who it suits

Oblong faces are the natural fit — a horizontal line across the forehead visually shortens a long face more effectively than any other single feature. Oval faces wear it easily. Square faces get a strong graphic pairing with the jaw.

Heart-shaped faces benefit because the fringe reduces the apparent width of the forehead. Round faces are the difficult case: adding a horizontal line to a face that is already wide tends to compound the effect.

The Caesar is a genuinely good solution for an uneven or receding front hairline, a pronounced widow's peak, or a high forehead — all of which the fringe simply covers. Fine hair works well because a blunt cut edge reads as denser than a textured one.

## Getting the cut right

Specify the fringe height. "Sitting about an inch above my eyebrows" gives your barber a reference point. Too low and it reads as a bowl cut; too high and the geometry disappears.

Ask for the top to be cut to an even length rather than graduated. That evenness is what distinguishes a Caesar from a crop, and it is what makes the fringe sit flat.

## Styling it day to day

Comb it forward while damp and let it dry in place. This is one of the few cuts here where a comb beats fingers — the flat, even surface is the point, and fingers introduce separation that works against it.

A trace of light cream holds the direction. Avoid clay and anything matte and gritty; those products exist to create separation, which is exactly what a Caesar is trying not to have.

## Living with it

Three to four weeks. The sides are forgiving but the fringe is not — a level line drawn across your forehead makes even a centimetre of growth obvious, and the balance between fringe and face shifts quickly.

This is the cut where a fringe-only trim between appointments pays off most. It takes a barber two minutes and effectively doubles the life of the haircut.
