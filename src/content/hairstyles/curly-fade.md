A curly fade puts defined curl on top and takes the sides down close. It is the highest-contrast pairing available for curly hair, and it has become one of the most-worn cuts in barbering because the two elements flatter each other so directly — smooth faded skin against dense, textured curl.

The fade also solves a practical problem. Curls at the sides of the head tend to grow outward rather than downward, which is what makes curly hair widen at the temples over time. Removing them entirely gives the curls above a clean shape to sit on.

## Who it suits

Round faces gain from the tight sides and the height on top. Square and diamond faces get their structure emphasised. Heart faces benefit from volume that sits above a narrow chin.

Oval faces carry it comfortably. Oblong faces should choose a mid rather than high fade — a high fade plus curl volume adds significant apparent height to an already long face.

Curly, coily and thick hair are the requirement. The whole cut depends on there being enough curl on top to contrast against a bare side, and loose waves produce a much subtler version.

## Getting the cut right

Two separate instructions. For the sides: fade height, and whether you want skin or a guard at the bottom. For the top: cut dry, and keep the volume.

Cutting curly hair dry matters here as much as on any curly cut. Curls shrink as they dry, and a top cut wet will be dramatically shorter than intended once it dries — which, over a high fade, changes the whole balance of the cut.

Do not let anyone use thinning shears on the top. Thinned curls create short frizzy pieces that break out of the curl pattern.

## Styling it day to day

Apply curl cream or leave-in to soaking wet hair while you are still in the shower, then scrunch once and stop touching it. Diffuse on low heat and low speed if you need it dry quickly; high heat on curls produces frizz within minutes.

On the second day, spray with water rather than adding more product. Curls revive with moisture; adding cream to already-product-laden hair just weighs them down.

Keep product off the faded sides. Bare or near-bare skin with curl cream on it looks greasy.

## Living with it

The fade sets the schedule: two weeks for a skin fade, three for a guard fade. The curls on top can go six weeks or more and often look their best in the middle of that range.

Booking a sides-only refresh between full cuts is the usual approach and it works well — the top rarely needs attention on the same cycle as the fade.
