The slick back is the simplest idea in this catalogue and one of the hardest to execute. All the hair is combed straight back from the hairline, in one direction, with a finish that shows some shine. There is no parting, no fringe, no texture — just direction and surface.

That simplicity is what makes it formal. Nothing is hidden and nothing is broken up, so the cut reads as controlled. It is also why it takes practice: with no texture to disguise anything, every unevenness in the comb-through is visible.

## Who it suits

Square and diamond faces are the natural fit. Pulling everything back exposes bone structure, and strong structure is what makes the look work. Oval faces carry it comfortably. Round faces do reasonably well, since the height at the front adds vertical, though a slight lift at the hairline helps more than a completely flat sweep.

Oblong faces are the weakest match — a fully exposed forehead lengthens an already long face. Heart faces have a similar issue with forehead width.

The honest caution is the hairline. A slick back puts it at the centre of the composition. Temple recession can look deliberate and fine; a substantially receded hairline will be the first thing anyone notices.

Thick straight hair is ideal. Wavy hair works with heat and product. Curly hair genuinely does not suit this shape.

## Getting the cut right

You need enough length on top to reach the crown when combed back — usually eight centimetres or more. Ask for the sides to be tapered with some weight left in them; very short sides under a slick back tip the look toward severe.

Ask your barber to keep some graduation from front to back. Hair cut to one length on top slicks back as a flat sheet; graduated hair slicks back with a slight lift at the front, which is much more flattering.

## Styling it day to day

Product goes on damp hair. Water-based pomade needs moisture to spread evenly, and applying it dry produces patchy shine and visible clumps.

Comb from the hairline straight back in a single motion rather than working in sections — sectioning creates parallel lines that look striped. Then ten seconds of warm air to set the shape. Heat does more for hold than doubling your pomade will.

## Living with it

The cut itself is forgiving: five to six weeks before the sides need attention, and the top can grow considerably before the shape suffers.

What it costs is daily. There is no way to slick hair back without wetting, combing and drying it, and that is five minutes every morning. If you want the same silhouette with less commitment, the medium swept back styled with matte cream and fingers is the relaxed cousin.
