Shoulder length is where men's long hair stops being about the cut and starts being about condition. The shape is simple — length to the shoulder with soft layers through the bottom third — and almost everything that determines how it looks happens between haircuts rather than during them.

It is also the last length that works without a genuine routine. Past the shoulder, hair needs tying, treating and planning around. At the shoulder it can still be washed, combed and left.

## Who it suits

Square and diamond faces gain from the softening effect of length around the jaw. Round faces benefit from the strong vertical, which is the single most effective way to narrow a round face. Oval faces have no constraints.

Heart faces work if some weight is kept around the chin. Oblong faces are the poor fit — this much vertical on an already long face is difficult to balance, and a shorter, wider shape serves better.

Thick hair is close to a requirement. The weight of shoulder-length hair pulls roots flat, so you need density to keep any volume at the crown. Wavy hair is ideal — the movement stops the length looking heavy. Fine hair genuinely struggles: the ends thin out to very little and the result reads as straggly rather than long.

## Getting the cut right

Ask for the ends to be taken clean and for soft layers only through the bottom third. Layers cut too high create width at the cheekbone that most men do not want, and they are slow to grow out.

Tell your barber not to touch the crown. Volume at the top is what stops long hair looking flat, and it is the first thing a barber used to cutting shorter styles will remove.

## Styling it day to day

Wash less. Daily washing strips the natural oils that keep long ends flexible, and at this length the ends are three years old. Two or three washes a week with conditioner every time is the standard.

Comb wet with conditioner in it, using a wide-tooth comb, working from the ends upward. Combing dry long hair breaks it. Air dry where possible; heat is the other main cause of damage at this length.

If you tie it back, vary where the tie sits and use a soft one. An elastic at the same point every day causes breakage exactly there, and it is one of the most common self-inflicted problems with long hair.

## Living with it

Twelve-week shaping cuts. The cut itself is undemanding.

What it costs is daily attention to condition rather than daily styling. Shoulder-length hair that is clean, conditioned and combed reads as deliberate; the same length dry and tangled reads as neglected. That distinction, not the cut, is what determines how the style is perceived.
