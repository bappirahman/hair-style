A quiff puts its height at the front and nowhere else. The first few centimetres of hair at the hairline are lifted up and swept back; behind that, the hair simply follows. It is the loosest of the swept-back shapes and by some margin the easiest to build.

That concentration is the practical advantage. A pompadour requires you to construct volume across the whole top of your head. A quiff requires you to lift one small section. The visual payoff is similar; the morning cost is not.

## Who it suits

This is one of the most face-shape-tolerant cuts here. Round faces get useful vertical height. Square faces get softness at the top to offset the jaw. Heart and diamond faces both benefit from volume that draws the eye upward.

Oval faces wear it without thought. Even oblong faces can manage a quiff provided the height is kept modest, because the shape is lower and less imposing than a pompadour.

Hair type is similarly forgiving. Thick hair gives the most substantial quiff. Wavy hair gives the most natural one. Fine hair — unusually for a volume-dependent cut — genuinely works, because you are only asking a small section to stand up rather than building a whole structure.

## Getting the cut right

Ask for graduation from front to back. The front should be your longest hair, reducing toward the crown. This is what allows the front to lift while the rest lies down, and it is the difference between a quiff and a generally long top.

Sides are your choice: a taper for a softer overall look, a fade for more contrast. Both work. The quiff itself doesn't care.

## Styling it day to day

Point the dryer straight upward at the front section while it is damp, then push the section back once it is dry. That is the whole technique. Thirty seconds, no brush required.

Finish with matte cream or a light clay, applied mostly to the front. A quiff should look like it moved into that position rather than being set there, so avoid anything that produces a rigid or glossy surface.

## Living with it

Comfortable at four to five weeks. The top gets better as it grows for the first few weeks; only the sides really need attention on a schedule.

When it stops working, it is usually because the front has grown past the point where it can lift — hair that is too long falls forward under its own weight. That is the signal, and it is a slow one.
