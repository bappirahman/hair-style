The wolf cut takes the seventies shag — heavy layers, volume at the crown, movement everywhere — and crosses it with a mullet's longer back. The layers are aggressive and deliberately visible. The outline is left unfinished, with wispy ends rather than a clean perimeter.

It became one of the defining shapes of the decade because it does something most cuts do not: it makes hair look bigger and more textured than it is, without needing a blow-dry to achieve it. The volume is cut in rather than styled in.

## Who it suits

Round faces do well, because crown volume adds vertical and the layers around the face create angles. Square faces get their corners broken up by the movement. Diamond faces benefit from volume above the cheekbones rather than at them.

Oval faces have no issue. Heart faces work if the shortest layers start below the cheekbone. Oblong faces are the weakest match — crown volume on a long face adds height where it is not wanted, though a flatter version with the layers starting lower can work.

Wavy and curly hair are the natural home. The layers separate on their own and the whole shape falls into place. Thick straight hair works with styling help. Fine hair is difficult: the layers remove weight from hair that has little to spare, and the ends can look sparse rather than wispy.

## Getting the cut right

This cut demands a barber or stylist who has done it before. The layering is aggressive and specific — short layers at the crown, progressively longer through the mid-lengths, longest at the back — and a conservative interpretation produces a shapeless long haircut.

Ask for the ends to be left wispy rather than cut to a clean line. A blunt perimeter on a wolf cut fights everything else about it.

Say explicitly that you do not want it tidy. Barbers are trained to produce clean shapes and will default to one unless told otherwise.

## Styling it day to day

A diffuser is worth owning for this cut. Attach it, tip your head to the side, and scrunch the hair upward toward the scalp while drying. That builds the crown volume the layers were cut to support.

Sea salt spray or a light mousse on damp hair first. Texture powder at the crown if you need more lift. Leave the ends alone entirely — product on wispy ends makes them stringy, which is the difference between deliberate and neglected.

## Living with it

Eight weeks, and this one is not flexible. The layers are the shape. Once they grow out by more than a few centimetres the structure collapses, the volume drops, and what is left reads as hair that has simply not been cut.

That is the trade for a cut that needs so little daily effort: the effort is front-loaded into the appointment, and the appointment has to happen.
