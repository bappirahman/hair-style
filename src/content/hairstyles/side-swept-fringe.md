A side swept fringe travels diagonally across the forehead instead of hanging straight down. It is the softest way to wear a fringe, and the asymmetry does useful work: a diagonal line is more flattering on most faces than a horizontal one, because it introduces movement rather than a boundary.

The version worth having is short — sitting above or at the eyebrow, sweeping cleanly. The long, gelled, eye-covering interpretation from the late 2000s is genuinely dated, and it is worth being clear with your barber that you do not want it.

## Who it suits

Very broadly. Round faces gain from the diagonal, which breaks circularity in a way that a straight fringe cannot. Oblong faces get the forehead shortened. Square faces get their corners softened. Heart faces get the forehead width interrupted asymmetrically, which is more flattering than a symmetrical fringe.

Oval faces are fine. There is no face shape this actively fights.

Fine hair works well because a sweep concentrates hair along a line and reads as denser than the same hair hanging straight. Thick hair needs internal weight removed so the fringe lies down rather than standing off the forehead. Wavy hair is good. Tight curls cannot sweep and want a different shape.

## Getting the cut right

The direction should follow your natural growth. Comb your wet hair straight down and see which way the front settles — that is your direction, and sweeping against it means a fight every morning.

Ask for graduation, so the fringe is longest at the side it comes from and shorter where it lands. A fringe cut level and then swept sideways sits as a diagonal slab; a graduated one sits as a sweep.

If you have a cowlick at the front, tell your barber. A side sweep is usually the best available answer to one, because it works with the direction rather than against it.

## Styling it day to day

Dry it in the direction of the sweep. Point the airflow across your forehead the way the fringe should travel, while the hair is still damp. That sets the root direction, which is what actually controls a fringe — the ends follow.

Use a cream rather than a clay. A sweep needs to lie down and matte grit works against that. Very little is required.

## Living with it

Six weeks or more. As it grows the sweep simply gets longer and travels further across, which stays wearable for a long time.

The point to revisit it is when the fringe reaches your eye on the far side. At that stage it stops sweeping and starts hanging, which is the version nobody is after.
