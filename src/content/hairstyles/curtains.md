Curtains are a centre parting with the hair falling forward on both sides of the face. The style peaked in the mid-nineties, disappeared almost completely for twenty years, and came back so thoroughly that it is now one of the most requested medium-length looks in barbering.

The current version is softer than the original. Where nineties curtains were often blow-dried into two smooth, symmetrical wings, the modern interpretation is looser, less even, and more likely to be worn slightly messy. The parting is still central; everything else has relaxed.

## Who it suits

Round faces gain the most. Two vertical panels of hair either side of the face narrow it visually — exactly the opposite effect of a fringe. Square faces get their corners softened. Diamond faces do well since the hair falls over the widest part of the cheekbones.

Oval faces are fine. Heart faces can find that curtains emphasise a narrow chin, since the hair frames inward toward it. Oblong faces are the weakest fit: a centre parting adds vertical emphasis to an already long face.

Wavy hair is the natural home — the movement keeps the two sides from looking like flat panels. Straight hair works and gives the cleaner, more nineties version. Fine hair is well served because the parting creates the impression of density along the line. Thick hair needs weight removed or the curtains become heavy drapes.

## Getting the cut right

Length is the requirement: each side needs to reach at least your eyebrow. Below that, hair parted centrally sticks out sideways rather than falling.

Ask for the front to be graduated slightly shorter than the sides so the curtains frame rather than curtain. And ask for the sides to be kept soft — a tapered or faded side under curtains creates a hard contrast that fights the style's whole relaxed character.

## Styling it day to day

Part it while wet. A centre parting is fighting whatever direction your hair naturally grows, and it will not hold if you set it dry.

Dry each side outward, pointing the airflow away from the parting, so the hair falls rather than lying flat against the scalp. Sea salt spray for movement, then a light cream if you need any hold at all. Clay and wax turn curtains into two solid panels, which is the main way this style goes wrong.

## Living with it

Six to eight weeks between cuts, and it genuinely improves during that time. The extra length makes the curtains fall better.

The hard part is not maintenance, it is arrival. Growing into curtains from short hair takes three to five months and there is an unavoidable phase around weeks six to twelve where the hair is too long to be short and too short to part. Keeping the sides tidy through it helps more than anything else.
