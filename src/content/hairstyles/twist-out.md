A twist out is a styling technique rather than a cut. Damp, moisturised hair is separated into sections, each section is twisted from root to tip, and the twists are left to dry completely before being unravelled. What comes out is a defined, elongated version of your natural coil pattern with substantially less shrinkage.

The appeal is definition without heat. A twist out gives you visible, consistent curl formation using only moisture, product and time, which is the least damaging way to get there.

## Who it suits

The elongation means it reads longer and less wide than an afro, so it works well on faces that a wide round shape does not flatter. Oblong faces get useful width; square faces get softened; oval faces have no constraints.

Round faces do better with a twist out than a full afro, because the elongated coils add vertical rather than horizontal. Heart and diamond faces work well.

The requirement is coily or tightly curly hair. Looser curls will twist but the definition gained is minimal, since the natural pattern is already elongated.

## Getting the cut right

The cut matters less than the technique, but shape still helps. Ask for the top to be shaped evenly and left long enough to twist — usually at least eight centimetres. Sides tapered or faded depending on the contrast you want.

Cut dry. This is non-negotiable with coily hair, and doubly so here, since a twist out changes the apparent length again.

## Styling it day to day

Work on damp, moisturised hair. Section it — smaller sections give tighter, more defined results; larger sections give looser, chunkier ones. Apply curl cream to each section, then two-strand twist from root to tip.

Let it dry completely. This is where most twist outs fail. Damp twists have not set, and unravelling them returns the hair to its natural pattern with added frizz for the trouble. Overnight is the safe answer.

Unravel with a little oil on your fingertips, working gently and avoiding separating the coils too much. Separating aggressively adds volume but costs definition.

## Living with it

The style lasts three to four days. Re-twisting just the front sections at night and covering with a satin durag extends it and keeps the most visible part sharp.

The cut underneath runs on a six-week cycle. As with all coily hair, the real routine is moisture and overnight protection rather than styling product.
