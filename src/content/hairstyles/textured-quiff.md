A textured quiff is the same shape as a standard quiff — lift at the front, swept loosely back — with the surface broken apart rather than smoothed. The top is point-cut so the ends sit at varying lengths, and the styling separates it into visible pieces instead of a single mass.

That difference matters more than it sounds. A smooth quiff has to be executed cleanly or it looks like it went wrong. A textured one was never meant to be even, which makes it the most forgiving front-volume cut available.

## Who it suits

Practically every face shape. Round faces get vertical height; square faces get softness against the jaw; heart and diamond faces get the eye drawn upward. Oval faces have no issue. Even oblong faces can wear it if the height stays modest, because broken texture reads as less tall than a smooth swept mass of the same height.

Hair type is where it really opens up. Thick hair benefits from the internal weight removal that point-cutting provides. Wavy hair is the natural home — natural movement and deliberate separation reinforce each other. Straight hair works but needs salt spray to have anything to separate. Loose curls are excellent.

## Getting the cut right

Ask for the top to be point-cut rather than blunt-cut. That single technical instruction is what produces the texture; everything else is styling.

Be careful about how much weight comes out. Over-texturing leaves the ends thin and the shape hollow, which is the most common way this cut fails. If your hair is fine, say so and ask for a light hand.

## Styling it day to day

Texture must exist before it can be styled. Sea salt spray on damp hair adds the grit that lets pieces hold apart — without it, clay applied to smooth hair just produces heavier smooth hair.

Scrunch the front upward while drying rather than brushing it. A dusting of texture powder at the roots lifts and grips simultaneously and is worth having if your hair is fine. Finish with a small amount of matte clay pressed in from underneath.

## Living with it

Four to six weeks, and it improves for the first two or three. Because the shape was deliberately imprecise, growth adds texture rather than removing definition.

The end point is the same as any quiff: when the front is too long to lift and starts falling forward instead. That takes longer here than with a smooth quiff, because a slightly-too-long textured front still reads as intentional mess.
