A burst fade radiates outward from the ear in a semicircle, like light from a point source. The hair immediately around the ear is shortest, and it lengthens in every direction from there — forward toward the temple, upward toward the crown, and backward toward the nape. What it does *not* do is continue around the back of the head, and that omission is the whole idea.

Because the nape keeps its length, a burst fade is the natural partner for anything with weight at the back: a mullet, a longer neck section, a shag. It gives you the crisp, faded look around the ear without committing to the short-all-round outline that a standard fade forces.

## Who it suits

The burst reads well on almost any face shape, because it barely alters the head's overall outline — it removes a small, specific area rather than a whole band. Oval, square, heart and diamond faces all wear it comfortably.

It is genuinely at its best on textured hair. The cut was popularised on curly and coily hair, where the contrast between faded skin around the ear and dense curl above it is dramatic in a small area. That said, it has crossed over completely, and straight-haired versions are now common — they simply need a bit more length left above the burst so the contrast has something to work against.

If your ears stick out, be aware that a burst fade draws a circle around each one. Some people love the effect; if you would rather not, a low taper achieves similar tidiness without the spotlight.

## Getting the cut right

The key instruction is what happens behind the ear. Barbers who cut a lot of standard fades will instinctively continue the taper around the nape, which turns a burst fade into an ordinary one. Say explicitly: "keep the weight at the back, don't take it round."

The second decision is the radius of the burst — how far the short section extends from the ear. A tight burst is subtle and grows out easily. A wide one is a statement and reads as a deliberate design.

## Styling it day to day

The top and back are where the styling happens. If you have natural texture, work a curl cream through damp hair and either diffuse it or let it air dry — the burst fade's whole logic is contrast between smooth and textured, so suppressing your texture defeats it.

On straight hair, a matte paste worked back and up gives the top enough shape to balance the faded ear area. Keep the finish dry rather than glossy.

## Living with it

Burst fades age gracefully. Because the faded area is small and surrounded by length, regrowth blurs into its neighbours rather than forming a visible line across the head. Three to four weeks is a realistic cycle, and the cut still looks intentional at five.

The part that goes first is the sharp edge just in front of the ear. When that starts to look soft rather than defined, you are due — but you have time.
