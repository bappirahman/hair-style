A side part is hair divided along a line and swept across. It is the most conventional men's hairstyle there is, which is exactly why it keeps working: it has been read as smart in essentially every decade of the last century, and it will still be read that way in ten years.

What separates a good side part from a mediocre one is almost never product. It is whether the parting sits where the hair actually wants to split, and whether the cut supports that line rather than fighting it.

## Who it suits

Very broadly. Round faces do well because a side part introduces a diagonal, which breaks up circularity better than any symmetrical shape. Square faces get softened. Oblong faces gain from horizontal movement across the head. Heart faces get useful asymmetry across the forehead.

Oval faces have no constraints. Diamond faces are the only slightly awkward fit, since a side sweep can add width right at the cheekbones.

Fine hair is genuinely well served — sweeping across creates more apparent coverage than sweeping back, and the parting gives the eye something deliberate to look at. Thick hair works with weight removed underneath so the swept side doesn't balloon. Very curly hair does not part cleanly and is better in a different shape.

## Getting the cut right

The whole cut is the parting, so ask your barber to find your natural one. The method is simple: comb the wet hair straight back, then push it forward with a flat palm and let it fall. Wherever it splits is the natural line.

Ask for the cut to be graduated so the swept side has somewhere to go. Hair cut to a single length parts as two equal slabs; graduated hair parts and falls.

You can also ask for a hard part — a fine line shaved along the parting. It is sharp and it commits you to a two-week cycle. Most people do not need it.

## Styling it day to day

Comb the parting in while the hair is damp. A parting established in dry hair almost never holds, because the hair has already set in a different direction.

Then dry along the line of the part, pointing the airflow so the hair lies away from the parting on both sides. Finish with a light cream. A side part is one of the few cuts here where a little shine is appropriate rather than dated.

## Living with it

Six weeks or more, which makes it one of the lowest-maintenance styles here. The parting is created by the cut and it stays where the cut put it as the hair grows.

The one thing that undermines it is bulk building up on the swept side. When the hair starts to sit above the parting rather than falling away from it, it is time for some weight to come out.
