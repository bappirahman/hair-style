A buzz cut is one clipper length taken over the entire head. There is no gradient, no parting, no styling and no product. It is the most honest haircut available, in both the good and the difficult sense of that word.

Its appeal is partly practical and partly aesthetic. Practically, it removes hair from your daily routine entirely. Aesthetically, it strips away framing and puts all the emphasis on bone structure, which on the right head is extremely flattering.

## Who it suits

This is the one cut where face shape advice is worth taking seriously, because there is nothing to compensate with. Oval faces carry it easily. Square and diamond faces often look their best in it, because the removal of hair leaves a strong jaw or defined cheekbones doing all the work.

Round faces are the difficult case: with no height on top and no width removed at the sides, a round face reads rounder. Heart-shaped faces can find the exposed forehead unbalancing. Neither is a hard no, but both benefit from going a guard or two longer to keep some softness.

Hair type is almost irrelevant, which is unusual. Thinning hair is arguably better served by a buzz than by any attempt to cover it — at a uniform short length, thin areas stop being visible as thin areas.

What genuinely matters is head shape, scalp condition and ears. A buzz cut shows all three exactly as they are.

## Getting the cut right

Choose a guard number and be specific. A two — around six millimetres — is the standard and the most forgiving. A one is distinctly closer and shows scalp contour clearly. A three or four keeps enough colour to hide minor unevenness and is the sensible starting point if you have never had one.

Ask for the neckline to be tapered rather than blocked. A hard squared nape on an otherwise uniform cut looks incongruous, and it grows into a shelf.

Also decide whether to keep your natural hairline or have it lined up. Natural is softer and grows out invisibly. A line-up is sharper and needs redoing every couple of weeks.

## Styling it day to day

There isn't any, and that is the appeal. What replaces styling is scalp care. At this length your scalp is part of the look — dryness, flaking and sunburn are all visible in a way they are not under longer hair. A light moisturiser and sunscreen in summer are worth the thirty seconds.

Most people find a buzz cut sits better with some facial hair. Removing all the hair above your face and none below it puts a lot of weight on the lower half; a few days of stubble rebalances it.

## Living with it

There is no shape to lose, so nothing goes wrong. A buzz simply gets longer, and at some point around four or five weeks it stops looking like a choice and starts looking like neglect.

Because it is a single guard length with no blending, it is also the one cut here you can genuinely do yourself. A set of clippers and a mirror will get you a perfectly acceptable result, which over a year is a meaningful saving.
