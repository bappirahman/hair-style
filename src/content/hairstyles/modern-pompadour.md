The modern pompadour keeps the classic's essential move — front length swept up and back — and changes almost everything around it. The sides are faded rather than tapered, so the contrast is sharper. The volume is lower and deliberately less uniform. The finish is matte instead of glossy.

The effect is a shape that reads as current rather than referential. A classic pompadour always carries a hint of period costume; the modern version does not, and it is considerably less work.

## Who it suits

Round faces get the same benefit as with the classic — vertical height where it is useful — with the added advantage that faded sides remove width more aggressively than tapered ones. Square and heart faces both work well.

Oval faces carry it easily. Oblong faces should keep the volume modest; the high fade already lengthens the head, and stacking a tall front on top compounds it.

Thick and wavy hair are the strongest matches. Wavy hair is arguably better suited to the modern version than the classic, because the intentional looseness works with natural movement rather than against it. Fine hair manages better here than with a classic pompadour, since the target volume is lower.

## Getting the cut right

Specify both halves. "High fade on the sides, and keep the front long enough to sweep up and back" gives your barber the two pieces of information they need.

Ask for the top to graduate — longest at the front, reducing toward the crown. This is the same requirement as the classic and it is what allows the front to fold back rather than standing straight up.

Decide on the fade height while you are there. A mid fade gives a softer, more wearable version; a high fade gives the graphic contrast most people picture.

## Styling it day to day

Blow dry back using your fingers rather than a brush. Fingers produce the looser, less uniform volume the modern version wants; a round brush produces a classic pompadour whether you meant to or not.

A matte cream is the right product. Pomade adds shine, and shine is the single element that pushes this look back toward the retro version. Once the shape is set, pull a few pieces out of place. It is supposed to look worn in.

## Living with it

The two halves diverge again. The top is forgiving and looks good for six weeks. The fade does not — two weeks for a high fade, three for a mid.

That mismatch is the cut's main practical cost, and it is worth deciding at the outset whether the fade contrast is worth a fortnightly appointment. A modern pompadour over a mid taper gets you most of the way there on a four-week cycle.
