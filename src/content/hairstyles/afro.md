An afro is coily hair grown out and shaped rather than cut short. The silhouette — full, rounded, symmetrical — is created entirely by shaping, because hair does not grow evenly and left alone it produces an uneven mass rather than a shape.

Everything about wearing one well comes down to two things: regular shaping, and moisture. The cut is a small part of it.

## Who it suits

Long and oblong faces gain from the width the shape adds. Square faces are softened by the round silhouette. Heart and diamond faces benefit from volume distributed evenly rather than concentrated at the top.

Oval faces have no constraints. Round faces are the trickiest — a round shape above a round face compounds it — though a taller, slightly narrower afro rather than a wide one addresses this well.

This cut requires coily or tightly curly hair. There is no substitute for the natural texture.

## Getting the cut right

Ask for shaping rather than cutting. The goal is an even silhouette, and that means taking small amounts from wherever the shape is uneven rather than reducing overall length.

Cut dry, always. Coily hair shrinks by up to seventy-five percent when wet, and no barber can judge a shape they cannot see.

Ask for the outline to be defined at the hairline and around the ears. A shaped afro with a clean line-up reads as deliberate; the same volume with a soft, undefined perimeter reads as grown out.

## Styling it day to day

Pick from the roots outward, in sections, with a wide-toothed afro pick. Working from the ends creates tangles and pulls the hair flat. Pick when the hair is moisturised — dry picking is the main cause of breakage in coily hair.

Leave-in conditioner daily, not weekly. Coily hair is structurally prone to dryness because the natural oils secreted at the scalp cannot travel down a tightly coiled shaft. A hair butter or oil sealed over the leave-in keeps moisture in.

Protect it overnight. A satin durag or a satin pillowcase prevents the friction that causes both breakage and a flattened shape by morning. This single habit does more for an afro than any product.

## Living with it

Four to six weeks between shaping appointments. The length keeps increasing; only the evenness needs maintaining.

Expect significant shrinkage, and do not treat it as a problem — coily hair that shrinks is coily hair with healthy elasticity. Measure length stretched rather than at rest and the growth is far more visible than it appears day to day.
