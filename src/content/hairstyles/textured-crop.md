A textured crop takes the French crop's forward-fringe idea and removes the rigidity. The fringe is point-cut rather than cut straight across, so instead of one solid horizontal line you get a broken edge made of pieces at slightly different lengths. Weight is taken out through the top so the whole section sits with visible separation.

The result is softer and considerably more forgiving. Where a blunt crop demands precision — the fringe line either sits right or it doesn't — a textured crop was never precise, so growth and bad hair days barely register.

## Who it suits

This is one of the broadest-fitting cuts here. Round faces do notably better than with a blunt crop, because the broken fringe and the retained height on top add vertical rather than horizontal emphasis. Oblong faces still get the shortening effect of a fringe without the hard line.

Square, oval and heart shapes all wear it comfortably. It is genuinely difficult to make this cut look wrong on a face.

Thick hair is the strongest match, because point-cutting is a weight-removal technique and thick hair needs the help. Wavy hair is arguably even better — the natural movement does half the work. Straight fine hair can carry it if the barber is careful not to remove too much; over-textured fine hair looks sparse rather than piecey.

## Getting the cut right

The instruction is about technique, not length. "Point-cut the fringe so it's piecey rather than blunt, and take some weight out through the top" tells your barber exactly what to do with the scissors.

Be careful about how much weight comes out. Over-texturing is the most common failure with this cut — it leaves the ends wispy and the shape hollow. If you have fine hair, say so and ask them to go lightly.

## Styling it day to day

Texture has to be built before it can be styled. Sea salt spray on damp hair adds grit that gives the pieces something to hold onto; clay applied to smooth hair just makes smooth hair heavier.

Rough dry with your fingers only — no comb at any point. Then a small amount of clay warmed between dry palms and pressed into the fringe from underneath, lifting rather than smoothing. The finish should look like the hair fell that way.

## Living with it

Four to five weeks, comfortably. Because the shape is deliberately imprecise, growth reads as more texture rather than as loss of shape. Many people prefer it at week three than on day one.

The limit is fringe length. When the pieces start reaching your eyes rather than your forehead, the balance tips and the cut needs revisiting.
