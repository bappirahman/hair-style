An undercut is defined by what it doesn't do. The sides are clipped to one short length, the top is left long, and there is no gradient between them — just a step. Every other short-back-and-sides cut in this catalogue blends. This one deliberately refuses to.

That refusal is what gives it its graphic quality and also what made it feel over-exposed by the middle of the last decade. What has survived, and survived well, is the softer interpretation: the same structural disconnection, but styled loosely rather than plastered down, so the step reads as shape rather than as statement.

## Who it suits

Round faces do well, because the tight sides remove width and the length on top adds height. Square and diamond faces get their bone structure emphasised. Oval faces, as ever, can wear it however they like.

Heart-shaped and oblong faces are harder. A heart face already carries width at the top, and stacking length there can unbalance it. An oblong face gets stretched further by the added vertical.

Thick straight hair is the classic fit — there needs to be enough weight on top to hold a shape once it is eight or more centimetres long. Fine hair struggles, because the disconnection exposes the sides completely and fine hair on top rarely has the body to justify the contrast. Very curly hair is possible but wants a much shorter top or the proportions become unwieldy.

## Getting the cut right

The instruction is short and specific: "number two on the sides, no blend, keep the top long." If your barber starts blending, you are getting a fade.

The one decision worth thinking about is where the disconnection line sits. Lower is softer and grows out more forgivingly. Higher is the graphic version and needs the top to be genuinely long to balance it.

## Styling it day to day

This cut depends on styling more than any other here, because a long top section over short sides will simply collapse without help. Blow dry back and up with a vent brush — this is not optional, it is most of the work.

Then layer: a cream through the length for shape, and a small amount of clay at the roots for staying power. Decide whether you are wearing it straight back or with a side parting, and commit. An undercut that is neither reads as unfinished.

## Living with it

The two halves run on different clocks. The sides need refreshing every three weeks to keep the disconnection sharp. The top can be left for two or three months, and often looks better with the extra length.

Many people book a "sides only" appointment between full cuts. It is quick, cheap, and it is the whole reason this cut is more manageable than its dramatic appearance suggests.
