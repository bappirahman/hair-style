Flow is hair grown past the ears and worn pushed back behind them. It sits between a medium swept back and genuinely long hair, and it is characterised more by attitude than by technique — the whole point is that it looks grown rather than cut.

That does not mean it is uncut. Flow that has genuinely never been shaped goes shapeless: bulk builds at the sides, the outline widens, and the hair sticks out rather than falling. The cut is invisible, but it exists.

## Who it suits

Square and diamond faces gain from the softening effect around the jaw. Round faces benefit from the vertical fall at the sides. Oval faces have no constraints.

Heart faces work if some length is kept around the chin. Oblong faces are the weakest fit — the vertical fall lengthens an already long face, though the width behind the ears offsets it somewhat.

Wavy hair is the natural home; the movement is most of the look. Thick straight hair works and gives a sleeker version. Fine hair struggles at this length because the weight pulls the roots flat, though a shorter interpretation can work.

## Getting the cut right

Ask for weight to be taken out underneath rather than length off the ends. That is the instruction that keeps flow from becoming a heavy triangle. Internal thinning at the sides — done carefully, not with thinning shears through the whole head — lets the hair tuck behind the ears rather than pushing out.

Ask for the shape to work with your ears. Hair that tucks is hair that stays where you put it; hair cut without reference to that will spend all day escaping.

## Styling it day to day

Push it behind your ears while it is damp. Hair dried in position stays in position, and this single habit is most of the styling.

Sea salt spray for texture, air dry, and that is generally the routine. A little oil through the ends if they are dry. Product beyond that works against the loose movement that defines the look.

## Living with it

Eight weeks between shaping cuts. The shape is forgiving and the length improves it.

The awkward part is arriving. Growing from a short cut to flow means passing through a phase — usually weeks six to fourteen — where the hair is long enough to stick out at the sides and too short to tuck. Regular thinning through that period and a light cream to keep it flat is the standard way through it. It is worth the wait, because past that point this is one of the lowest-effort ways to wear hair.
