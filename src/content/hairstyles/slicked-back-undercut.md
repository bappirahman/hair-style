Take an undercut, grow the top long enough to reach the crown, comb it straight back, and finish with shine. That is the slicked back undercut, and it is the most formal shape in this catalogue — the cut that reads as *dressed* rather than merely cut.

The structural logic is sound. Short sides mean the swept-back top has nothing competing with it, so the sweep reads cleanly rather than merging into side volume. It is a more architecturally honest way to wear hair back than most.

## Who it suits

Round faces benefit substantially: the tight sides remove width and the backward sweep adds visible height at the front. Square and diamond faces get their structure emphasised. Oval faces carry it without effort.

Oblong faces should be careful — sweeping hair back exposes the full forehead and lengthens the face further. Heart faces have the same issue with forehead width.

Thick straight hair is the ideal. The top needs weight to stay back, and it needs to be straight enough to comb into an even surface. Wavy hair works with more product and heat. Curly hair genuinely does not — combing curls straight back requires either chemical straightening or a fight you will lose by lunchtime.

Receding hairlines are the honest caution. Sweeping everything backward puts the hairline centre stage.

## Getting the cut right

Ask for the top to be long enough to reach the crown when combed back. That is the functional requirement, and it is easier for a barber to work with than a length in centimetres.

Keep the sides at a two rather than shorter. Very short sides under a slicked top can look severe in a way that tips the whole thing toward costume.

## Styling it day to day

This is the one cut here that wants product on damp rather than dry hair. Work a water-based pomade through evenly, comb straight back, then dry it in that position. The drying step is what people skip and it is the step that determines whether the shape survives past lunchtime.

Use a fine comb. The visible comb lines are part of the look, and finger-styling produces something entirely different.

## Living with it

The sides run on a three-week cycle like any undercut. The top can go months.

What this cut actually costs is daily time. There is no version of a slicked back undercut that works without combing and drying, and five minutes every morning is the real price. If that is not appealing, the same cut styled with matte clay and finger-swept back gives you eighty percent of the shape for a third of the effort.
