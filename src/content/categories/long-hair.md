Long hair is the one category here where the cut is a minor part of the outcome. What determines how it looks is condition, and condition is decided between appointments rather than during them.

The cutting decision that does matter is layering. Long hair without layers hangs as a single triangular mass — narrow at the crown, wide at the ends, with no movement anywhere. **Long layered hair** removes weight at varying depths so the hair falls in sections and follows the shape of the head.

**Shoulder length** is the practical limit for most men before the routine becomes genuinely involved. The **man bun** is less a cut than a way of wearing one, and the most useful thing to do with hair past the shoulder. The **wolf cut** takes the opposite approach — aggressive, visible layers and a deliberately unfinished outline.

The commitment worth understanding upfront is time. Reaching jaw length from a short cut takes eighteen months to two years, with an awkward phase in the middle that layers and a centre parting are the standard way through.
