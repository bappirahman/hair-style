Slicked-back styles share one idea: hair moved in a single direction and finished with a surface rather than a texture. There is no fringe and usually no break — just direction and shine.

That simplicity is what makes them formal, and what makes them demanding. With no texture to disguise anything, every unevenness in the comb-through shows, and the hairline is placed squarely at the centre of the composition.

The **slick back** takes everything straight back. The **side part** divides along a line and sweeps across, which is more conventional, more forgiving of a receding hairline, and considerably lower effort. The **medium swept back** is the relaxed version of the first — same direction, finger-styled and matte rather than combed and glossy.

Length is the common requirement: enough on top to reach the crown when swept, usually eight centimetres or more. Curly hair does not suit this family; the amount of product needed to comb curls flat rarely looks good.
