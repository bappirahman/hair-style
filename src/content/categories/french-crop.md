The crop family shares one move: the hair on top is pushed forward rather than back or up, so it lands as a fringe across the forehead. Everything else — how long, how textured, how blunt — varies between them.

That single decision is why these cuts have dominated the last decade. Moving hair forward covers the temples and the hairline completely, which makes the crop the most effective answer in barbering to a receding hairline. Nothing is being compensated for, because nothing is exposed.

The **French crop** is the blunt version, with a defined fringe edge. The **textured crop** point-cuts that edge into separate pieces, which is softer, more forgiving and currently more popular. Both sit over short tapered or faded sides.

Crops flatter long and oblong faces particularly, because a horizontal line across the forehead shortens the face. Round faces do better with the textured version, where the broken edge avoids adding a hard horizontal.
