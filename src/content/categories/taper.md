A taper and a fade are frequently confused, and the distinction is worth knowing because it changes how often you have to sit in a barber's chair. A **fade** ends at bare or near-bare skin. A **taper** keeps a guard length throughout — it is a gradient between two short lengths rather than between hair and scalp.

The consequence is longevity. Because nothing is shaved, nothing regrows as visible stubble, and there is no hard line to blur. A taper simply gets a little fuller each week, which is why the **low taper fade** is the most-requested cut in barbering: it is the smallest change that reliably makes hair look cut, and it lasts five or six weeks.

Height works the same way as with fades. A **low taper** touches only the ear and neckline. A **mid taper** reaches the temple. A **high taper** climbs to the ridge for near-fade contrast on a three-week schedule. The **classic taper** sits apart from all of them — cut with scissors rather than clippers, longer, softer, and traditionally side-parted.
