Wavy hair sits between straight and curly and is frequently treated as neither — cut too short to wave, or blow-dried straight, or thinned until the pattern breaks apart. Cut and dried properly, it produces texture that no styling product replicates.

Two rules cover most of it. First, length: waves need around four centimetres to form a visible bend. Below that, wavy hair reads as slightly untidy straight hair. Second, **do not thin it** — thinning shears create short pieces that stick out of the wave pattern and read as frizz.

The **wavy crop** is the short version, forward-styled. **Beach waves** are medium length worn loose and undone. The **wavy quiff** uses the natural lift that wave provides to build front volume with almost no product.

The routine across all three is mostly about restraint: salt spray on damp hair, scrunch once, then leave it completely alone while it dries. Touching hair as it dries is what turns waves into frizz.
