An undercut is defined by an absence. Every other short-back-and-sides cut blends the sides into the top. An undercut refuses to — the sides are clipped to one length, the top is left long, and there is a visible step between them.

That refusal is the source of its graphic quality, and it is also why the very sharp version felt over-exposed by the middle of the last decade. What has survived is the softer interpretation: the same structural disconnection, worn loosely rather than plastered down, so the step reads as shape rather than statement.

The **undercut** is the standard version. The **disconnected undercut** exaggerates both halves — shorter sides, much longer top, harder line. The **slicked back undercut** combs the long top straight back with shine and is the most formal cut in this catalogue.

All three share a practical feature worth knowing: the two halves run on different schedules. The sides need refreshing every two to three weeks; the top can go months. Booking short sides-only appointments between full cuts is what makes them manageable.
