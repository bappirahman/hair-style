A quiff puts its height at the front and nowhere else. The first few centimetres of hair at the hairline are lifted and swept back; behind that, the hair simply follows. It is the loosest of the swept-back shapes and by a wide margin the easiest to build.

That concentration is the practical advantage over a pompadour. A pompadour asks you to construct volume across the whole top of your head. A quiff asks you to lift one small section, which is why it works on fine hair where a pompadour does not.

The **quiff** is the smooth version. The **textured quiff** point-cuts the top so it separates into pieces, which is more forgiving and currently more worn. The **wavy quiff** uses natural wave to supply the lift, which makes it the least effortful of the three.

All three are among the most face-shape-tolerant cuts here, and all three sit comfortably over either a taper or a fade.
