Curly hair follows different rules, and two of them account for most of what goes wrong. The first is shrinkage: curls can dry to half their wet length, or a quarter for tight coils, which is why curly hair should be **cut dry**. A barber cutting wet curls cannot see the shape they are making.

The second is that frizz is usually mechanical rather than a moisture problem. Touching curls as they dry, brushing them dry, or drying with a terry towel disrupts the curl pattern, and a disrupted curl is what frizz is.

The **curly top** keeps full volume above tapered sides. The **curly fade** takes the contrast further with a faded side, and is one of the most-worn cuts in barbering. The **afro** grows the length out and shapes it into a round silhouette. The **twist out** twists damp hair and unravels it dry for elongated, defined coils. The **curly fringe** brings the curl forward over the forehead.

Across all of them, the daily commitment is moisture rather than styling. Natural oils cannot travel down a coiled shaft, so leave-in conditioner is a routine step, not an occasional treatment.
