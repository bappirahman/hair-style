A fade is a gradient. The hair starts short — sometimes at bare skin — at the bottom of the sides and back, and lengthens progressively as it climbs. Everything else about a fade is a variation on two questions: how short the shortest part goes, and how high up the head the gradient travels.

Those two variables produce the whole family. A **low fade** keeps the gradient below the temple and reads as tidy rather than dramatic. A **mid fade** puts the transition at temple height, where it is visible from the front. A **high fade** carries it to the parietal ridge for maximum contrast. A **skin fade** takes the base to bare scalp regardless of height. A **drop fade** curves the line down behind the ear rather than running it level, and a **burst fade** radiates it around the ear in a semicircle while leaving the back long.

The trade-off across all of them is the same: more contrast means a shorter lifespan. A low fade holds its shape for a month; a high skin fade looks its best for a week.
