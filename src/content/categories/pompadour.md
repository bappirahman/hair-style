A pompadour is hair swept upward from the hairline and rolled back over itself into a rounded mass of volume. It is the most sculptural shape in common circulation and the one that most clearly has to be built rather than worn.

It has been through several distinct eras — eighteenth-century court, fifties rock and roll, the 2010s revival — and each interpreted the height differently. What has not changed is the requirement: real length at the front, graduated shorter toward the crown, and a blow dryer.

The **classic pompadour** is the full version, glossy and high, over tapered sides. The **modern pompadour** lowers the volume, fades the sides and finishes matte, which reads as contemporary rather than referential and takes about half as long to style.

Both add significant apparent height, which makes them excellent for round faces and difficult for long ones. And both are daily commitments — there is no wash-and-go version of this shape.
