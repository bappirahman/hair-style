Curtains part the hair down the middle and let it fall forward on both sides of the face. The style peaked in the mid-nineties, vanished for twenty years, and returned so completely that it is now one of the most requested medium-length looks in barbering.

The modern version is looser than the original. Where nineties curtains were blow-dried into two smooth symmetrical wings, the current interpretation is messier, less even and more likely to be worn slightly undone.

**Curtains** are the shorter version, falling to around the cheekbone. The **middle part** is longer, falling past the cheekbones and back along the sides. The **messy fringe** and **side swept fringe** are the unstructured relatives — forward-facing, deliberately imprecise, and among the lowest-effort styles here.

All of them are excellent for round faces, because two vertical panels of hair either side narrow the face rather than widening it. The main cost is arrival: growing into curtains from short hair takes three to five months.
